## 📖 Chapter 1: The Six Essential Nutrients & The 4-4-9 Rule

Your body requires six classes of nutrients to survive and thrive. Three are **macronutrients** (they provide usable energy measured in calories) and three are **micronutrients / regulators** (they do not provide energy, but enable every biochemical reaction in your body).

### 1.1 The Macronutrients (Energy Yielding)

| Nutrient | Primary Biological Function | Calories per Gram | Recommended % of Daily Intake | Healthy Whole-Food Sources |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Primary, fast-acting source of fuel for cellular respiration and the central nervous system. | **4 cal/g** | **45% – 65%** | Oats, brown rice, sweet potatoes, whole wheat, quinoa, berries, legumes |
| **Proteins** | Growth, structural tissue repair, muscle synthesis, enzyme production, and immune antibodies. | **4 cal/g** | **10% – 35%** | Eggs, chicken breast, fish, tofu, lentils, beans, Greek yogurt |
| **Fats (Lipids)** | Long-term energy storage, organ protection, cell membrane structure, absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). | **9 cal/g** | **20% – 35%** | Avocados, extra virgin olive oil, walnuts, chia seeds, wild salmon |

> **Crucial Rule:**  
> **1g Carbohydrate = 4 Calories**  
> **1g Protein = 4 Calories**  
> **1g Fat = 9 Calories**  
> *(Notice that fat is more than twice as calorically dense per gram as carbs or protein!)*

---

### 1.2 Step-by-Step Calorie & Macronutrient Calculations

On the test, you will be given grams of carbs, protein, and fat and asked to calculate total calories and percentages.

#### 🧮 Worked Example 1: Calculating Total Calories from a Meal
A snack contains:
* **30 grams of Carbohydrates**
* **10 grams of Protein**
* **8 grams of Fat**

**Step 1:** Multiply each macronutrient gram amount by its caloric density:
* Calories from Carbs = $30\text{ g} \times 4\text{ cal/g} = \mathbf{120\text{ calories}}$
* Calories from Protein = $10\text{ g} \times 4\text{ cal/g} = \mathbf{40\text{ calories}}$
* Calories from Fat = $8\text{ g} \times 9\text{ cal/g} = \mathbf{72\text{ calories}}$

**Step 2:** Add them together for total calories:
$$\text{Total Calories} = 120 + 40 + 72 = \mathbf{232\text{ calories}}$$

#### 🧮 Worked Example 2: Calculating Percentage of Calories from Fat
Using the snack above (232 total calories, 72 calories from fat):
$$\text{Percentage of Fat Calories} = \left(\frac{72}{232}\right) \times 100 \approx \mathbf{31.0\%}$$
*Is this within the healthy daily recommendation?*  
**Yes**, because the recommended range for fat is **20% to 35%**.

### 1.3 Detailed Nutrient Classifications

#### A. Carbohydrates: Simple vs. Complex vs. Fiber
1. **Simple Carbohydrates (Sugars / Monosaccharides & Disaccharides):**
   * *Examples:* Glucose, fructose, sucrose, dextrose, high fructose corn syrup.
   * *Effect:* Rapidly digested into the bloodstream, creating rapid spikes in blood glucose followed by insulin surges and energy crashes.
2. **Complex Carbohydrates (Starches / Polysaccharides):**
   * *Examples:* Brown rice, whole oats, sweet potatoes, whole grain breads.
   * *Effect:* Long molecular chains of glucose that take time to break down, providing sustained energy without steep insulin spikes.
3. **Dietary Fiber (Non-digestible Plant Carbohydrate):**
   * *Soluble Fiber:* Dissolves in water to form a gel-like substance; binds to cholesterol in the digestive tract to lower blood LDL cholesterol levels (found in oats, beans, apples).
   * *Insoluble Fiber:* Adds bulk to stool and speeds up transit through the intestines, preventing constipation, hemorrhoids, and colon disease (found in wheat bran, vegetables, seeds).
   * *Daily Goal:* **25–35 grams per day**. (Most Americans get fewer than 15g!).

#### B. Proteins: Complete vs. Incomplete & Amino Acids
* Proteins are built from **20 amino acids**.
* **9 Essential Amino Acids:** The human body *cannot* synthesize these; you must consume them in your diet.
* **11 Non-Essential Amino Acids:** The body can manufacture these internally from other nutrients.
* **Complete Protein:** Contains all 9 essential amino acids in adequate proportions (animal products: meat, eggs, poultry, fish, dairy; plus plant exceptions like **soy/tofu** and **quinoa**).
* **Incomplete Protein:** Lacks one or more essential amino acids (most plant proteins: beans, rice, nuts).
* **Complementary Proteins:** Combining two incomplete plant sources (e.g., **beans and rice**, or **peanut butter on whole wheat**) provides all 9 essential amino acids together.

#### C. Fats: Saturated, Unsaturated, and Trans Fats
1. **Unsaturated Fats (Healthy Fats):**
   * Chemical structure has one or more double bonds (liquid at room temperature).
   * *Monounsaturated:* Olive oil, avocados, almonds (raises HDL "good" cholesterol, lowers LDL).
   * *Polyunsaturated:* Omega-3 and Omega-6 fatty acids (salmon, flaxseeds, walnuts) supporting brain health and reducing inflammation.
2. **Saturated Fats (Limit intake to < 10% of total calories):**
   * Chemical structure has all carbon bonds saturated with hydrogen (solid at room temperature).
   * Found in fatty cuts of meat, butter, cheese, whole milk, palm oil, coconut oil.
   * High intake increases **LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein)** cholesterol, leading to arterial plaque buildup (**atherosclerosis**).
3. **Trans Fats (Artificial / Partially Hydrogenated Oils — Avoid Completely):**
   * Created industrially through **hydrogenation** (bubbling hydrogen gas through vegetable oil to extend shelf life).
   * The most hazardous fat: **Raises LDL ("bad") cholesterol while simultaneously lowering HDL ("good") cholesterol**.
   * Banned by the FDA in processed foods, but watch for "< 0.5g per serving" loopholes in ingredient lists containing "partially hydrogenated oil".

### 1.4 Micronutrients: Vitamins, Minerals, and Water

Micronutrients do not provide energy, but without them, energy production and bodily functions grind to a halt.

#### A. Vitamins
* **Water-Soluble Vitamins (Vitamin C and B-Complex):**
  * Dissolve in water and cannot be stored in the body in large quantities.
  * Excess is excreted in urine daily, so they must be replenished regularly.
  * *Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid):* Collagen synthesis, wound healing, antioxidant, immune function. Deficiency causes **scurvy**.
  * *B-Vitamins (B1, B2, B3, B6, B12, Folate):* Co-enzymes for cellular energy production (converting carbohydrates and proteins into ATP). Folate prevents neural tube birth defects.
* **Fat-Soluble Vitamins (Vitamins A, D, E, K):**
  * Stored in the body's adipose (fat) tissue and liver.
  * Excessive consumption of supplements can lead to **toxicity (hypervitaminosis)**.
  * *Vitamin A:* Vision (rhodopsin in the retina), skin health, immune defense. Deficiency causes night blindness.
  * *Vitamin D:* Promotes intestinal absorption of Calcium and Phosphorus for bone mineralization. Synthesized by skin under sunlight. Deficiency causes **rickets** in children and **osteomalacia/osteoporosis** in adults.
  * *Vitamin E:* Powerful antioxidant protecting cellular membranes against oxidative damage.
  * *Vitamin K:* Essential for **blood clotting** (prothrombin synthesis) and bone metabolism.

#### B. Essential Minerals
* **Calcium (Ca):** Builds bones and teeth; essential for muscle contraction and nerve transmission. Deficiency causes **osteoporosis** (porous, brittle bones).
* **Iron (Fe):** Core component of **hemoglobin** in red blood cells, which carries oxygen from the lungs to body tissues. Deficiency causes **iron-deficiency anemia** (fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath).
* **Sodium (Na):** Maintains fluid balance and nerve impulses. Recommended limit: **< 2,300 mg/day**. Excessive intake leads to water retention, elevated blood pressure, and **hypertension**.
* **Potassium (K):** Counteracts sodium, regulates fluid balance, heart rhythm, and muscle function. Abundant in bananas, potatoes, spinach.

#### C. Water: The Most Critical Nutrient
* Makes up **60% to 70%** of human body mass.
* Functions: Thermoregulation through perspiration, joint lubrication, waste removal via kidneys, nutrient transport, and saliva production.
* Dehydration of as little as 2% body mass impairs cognitive function, physical endurance, and temperature control.

## 🏷️ Chapter 2: Decoding the FDA Nutrition Facts Label

Reading a food label accurately is a core practical skill on Coach Scott's summative exam.

```text
+-----------------------------------------------------------+
| Nutrition Facts                                           |
| 4 servings per container                                  |
| Serving size               1 cup (240g)                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Amount per serving                                        |
| Calories                   200                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------- |
|                                          % Daily Value*   |
| Total Fat 8g                                      10%     |
|   Saturated Fat 1g                                 5%     |
|   Trans Fat 0g                                            |
| Cholesterol 0mg                                    0%     |
| Sodium 460mg                                      20%     |
| Total Carbohydrate 30g                            11%     |
|   Dietary Fiber 4g                                14%     |
|   Total Sugars 12g                                        |
|     Includes 10g Added Sugars                     20%     |
| Protein 5g                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vitamin D 2mcg  10%  •  Calcium 260mg 20%                 |
| Iron 1.8mg      10%  •  Potassium 235mg 5%                |
+-----------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1 The 4 Step Food Label Inspection Method

1. **Step 1: Check the Serving Size & Servings Per Container**  
   All the numbers on the label describe **ONE SERVING**, not the entire package.  
   *Question:* If you eat the **entire package** above (4 servings), how many calories did you consume?  
   $$\text{Calories} = 200\text{ calories/serving} \times 4\text{ servings} = \mathbf{800\text{ calories}}$$  
   $$\text{Sodium} = 460\text{ mg} \times 4 = \mathbf{1,840\text{ mg}}\text{ (almost an entire day's maximum!)}$$

2. **Step 2: Check the Calories**  
   Are the calories appropriate for your meal or snack goals? Standard Daily Values are based on a **2,000 calorie reference diet**.

3. **Step 3: Master the 5/20 Rule for % Daily Value (%DV)**  
   * **5% DV or less** = **LOW** in that nutrient.  
   * **20% DV or more** = **HIGH** in that nutrient.  
   * *Nutrients you want LOW (5% or less):* Saturated Fat, Trans Fat, Sodium, Added Sugars.  
   * *Nutrients you want HIGH (20% or more):* Dietary Fiber, Vitamin D, Calcium, Iron, Potassium.

4. **Step 4: Scrutinize the Ingredients List**  
   * Ingredients are listed in descending order by **weight** (the first ingredient makes up the largest proportion of the food).  
   * If sugar (or high fructose corn syrup, cane sugar, dextrose) is in the top 3 ingredients, the food is heavily processed and nutrient-poor.

## 🍽️ Chapter 3: USDA MyPlate & Dietary Guidelines for Americans

The USDA replaced the outdated Food Pyramid with **MyPlate** to provide a visual, easy-to-use guide for healthy meal proportions.

### 3.1 The 5 MyPlate Food Groups & Golden Rules

```text
           +--------------------+--------------------+
           |                    |                    |
           |      FRUITS        |      GRAINS        |
           |     (~20-25%)      |      (~30%)        |
           |   Focus on whole   |  Make half whole   |
           |       fruits       |      grains        |
           |                    |                    |
           +--------------------+--------------------+   +---------+
           |                    |                    |   |  DAIRY  |
           |    VEGETABLES      |     PROTEIN        |   | (~1 cup)|
           |      (~30%)        |      (~20%)        |   | Low-fat |
           | Vary your veggies  |   Vary protein     |   | or 1%   |
           |                    |      routine       |   +---------+
           +--------------------+--------------------+
```

1. **Make half your plate Fruits & Vegetables:**
   * Bright, colorful, nutrient-dense foods rich in vitamins, minerals, water, and fiber with low caloric density.
   * Prioritize whole fruits over fruit juices (juices lack fiber and spike blood sugar).
2. **Make at least half of your Grains WHOLE grains:**
   * A whole grain kernel contains all three anatomical parts:
     * **Bran:** Outer protective shell rich in dietary fiber and B vitamins.
     * **Endosperm:** Starchy middle carbohydrate layer.
     * **Germ:** Nutrient-dense embryo packed with Vitamin E, healthy fats, and antioxidants.
   * *Refined grains* (white bread, white rice, enriched flour) have had the bran and germ stripped away during milling, leaving only empty starchy endosperm.
3. **Vary your Protein routine:**
   * Lean meats (poultry, turkey), fish (omega-3s), eggs, nuts, seeds, and plant proteins (beans, peas, lentils, soy).
4. **Choose Low-Fat (1%) or Fat-Free Dairy (or fortified alternatives):**
   * Provides Calcium and Vitamin D without excessive saturated animal fat.

## ⚖️ Chapter 4: Metabolism, Energy Balance & Weight Dynamics

### 4.1 Key Metabolic Terms
* **Metabolism:** The sum of all chemical reactions in the body that convert food into energy and cellular structures.
* **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR):** The minimum number of calories your body burns at complete physical rest just to keep vital autonomic organs functioning (heart beating, lungs breathing, brain firing, cellular homeostasis).
* **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE):** The total calories burned throughout an entire day.  
  $$\text{TDEE} = \text{BMR} + \text{Thermic Effect of Food (TEF)} + \text{Physical Activity / Exercise}$$

### 4.2 The Principle of Energy Balance
Energy balance determines body mass over time:
1. **Caloric Equilibrium (Energy In = Energy Out):** Weight remains stable.
2. **Positive Energy Balance (Energy In > Energy Out):** Excess calories are converted into glycogen and stored as adipose tissue (weight gain).
3. **Negative Energy Balance (Energy In < Energy Out):** Calorie deficit forces the body to metabolize stored fat and glycogen for fuel (weight loss).

### 4.3 The 3,500 Calorie Rule for Weight Change
* **1 pound of body fat contains approximately 3,500 calories of stored energy.**
* To lose **1 pound of fat per week**, a person needs a cumulative deficit of **3,500 calories** over 7 days:
  $$\frac{3,500\text{ calories}}{7\text{ days}} = \mathbf{500\text{ calorie deficit per day}}$$
  *(This can be achieved by eating 250 fewer calories and burning 250 more calories through exercise daily).*
* Safe, sustainable medical weight loss recommendation: **1 to 2 pounds per week maximum**. Crash diets cause water loss, muscle wasting, and a drastic drop in BMR.

## 🧠 Chapter 5: Eating Disorders, Body Image & Disordered Eating

Eating disorders are serious, complex mental illnesses with severe physical consequences. They are **not** lifestyle choices or fads.

### 5.1 Clinical Eating Disorders Comparison

| Characteristic | Anorexia Nervosa | Bulimia Nervosa | Binge Eating Disorder (BED) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Behavioral Pattern** | Severe, obsessive self-starvation; extreme restriction of calorie intake; intense fear of gaining weight. | Recurrent episodes of uncontrollable binge eating followed by compensatory purging behaviors. | Recurrent episodes of eating massive quantities of food rapidly until painfully full, **without** purging. |
| **Purging Behaviors** | Rare (primarily restriction or extreme compulsive exercising). | **Yes:** Self-induced vomiting, misuse of laxatives, diuretics, or enemas. | **No compensatory purging behaviors.** |
| **Body Weight Profile** | Severely underweight (BMI well below normal reference ranges). | Often fluctuates within a normal or slightly overweight range (making it easy to conceal). | Typically overweight or clinically obese due to chronic caloric surpluses. |
| **Psychological Mindset** | Severe body dysmorphia (perceives self as overweight even when emaciated); perfectionism. | Deep shame, guilt, and self-disgust following binging episodes; obsession with weight and shape. | Profound feelings of distress, guilt, depression, and loss of control during and after binges. |
| **Dangerous Physical Health Complications** | • Bradycardia (dangerously slow heart rate)<br>• Hypotension (low blood pressure)<br>• Amenorrhea (loss of menstrual cycle)<br>• Bone density loss (osteopenia)<br>• Growth of lanugo (fine downy body hair to keep warm)<br>• Cardiac arrest / organ failure | • Severe electrolyte imbalance (potassium loss)<br>• Cardiac arrhythmia (fatal irregular heartbeat)<br>• Chronic esophageal tears & acid reflux<br>• Tooth enamel erosion from stomach hydrochloric acid<br>• Swollen salivary glands ("chipmunk cheeks") | • Severe obesity & visceral adiposity<br>• Type 2 diabetes mellitus<br>• High blood pressure & heart disease<br>• Chronic gallbladder and digestive tract disorders |

> **Important Concept: Eating Disorder vs. Disordered Eating**  
> *Disordered eating* refers to abnormal eating patterns (like skipping breakfast before a dance or trying an extreme temporary cleanse) that do not meet full DSM-5 clinical criteria. When the behavior becomes chronic, compulsive, and mentally destructive, it transitions into a clinical *eating disorder* requiring medical, nutritional, and psychological treatment.

## 🩺 Chapter 6: Diet-Related Chronic Diseases

Diet is a primary modifiable lifestyle factor. The chronic conditions tested on Coach Scott's summative include:

### 1. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
* **Definition:** Persistent resting blood pressure of $\ge 130/80\text{ mmHg}$.
* **Primary Dietary Cause:** Chronic high intake of **Sodium** ($> 2,300\text{ mg/day}$) from processed and packaged foods, combined with low potassium.
* **Mechanism:** Excess sodium draws water into the bloodstream, increasing blood volume and placing high mechanical stress on arterial walls.
* **Complications:** Strokes, heart attacks, kidney failure.

### 2. Atherosclerosis & Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
* **Definition:** Hardening and narrowing of arterial walls due to plaque buildup.
* **Primary Dietary Cause:** High consumption of **Saturated Fats** and **Trans Fats**, which raise circulating **LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein) cholesterol**.
* **Mechanism:** Excess LDL particles oxidize and accumulate under endothelial lining of coronary arteries, creating atherosclerotic plaques that restrict blood flow to cardiac muscle.
* **Complications:** Angina, heart attacks (myocardial infarction).

### 3. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
* **Definition:** A metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia (high blood sugar) caused by cellular **insulin resistance**.
* **Primary Dietary Cause:** Chronic consumption of refined sugars, simple carbs, high-calorie processed diets, lack of dietary fiber, and physical inactivity leading to obesity.
* **Mechanism:** The pancreas produces insulin to move glucose from blood into cells. Over time, constant sugar surges overwhelm receptors, causing cells to become resistant to insulin. The pancreas burns out and blood sugar remains dangerously elevated.
* **Complications:** Blindness (diabetic retinopathy), peripheral neuropathy, kidney failure, amputations.

### 4. Osteoporosis
* **Definition:** Progressive loss of bone mineral density, making bones porous, fragile, and prone to fractures.
* **Primary Dietary Cause:** Chronic deficiency of **Calcium** and **Vitamin D** during adolescent bone-building years (peak bone mass is established by age 20–25!).
* **Prevention:** Consuming calcium-rich dairy or fortified plant milks, getting regular sunlight (Vitamin D), and performing **weight-bearing physical exercise** (running, weightlifting).

## 🎯 Chapter 7: Practice Summative Exam Scenarios & Answer Keys

Test your mastery on these typical multi-part summative test items:

### Scenario 1: Food Label Analysis
**A cereal label displays:**
* Serving Size: $3/4\text{ cup}$
* Servings Per Container: $8$
* Calories: $160$
* Total Fat: $2\text{ g}$
* Sodium: $230\text{ mg}$ ($10\%\text{ DV}$)
* Total Carbohydrates: $34\text{ g}$
* Dietary Fiber: $1\text{ g}$ ($4\%\text{ DV}$)
* Added Sugars: $16\text{ g}$ ($32\%\text{ DV}$)
* Protein: $3\text{ g}$

1. **How many calories would Antony consume if he eats $1.5\text{ cups}$ of cereal?**
   * *Solution:* $1.5\text{ cups} = 2\text{ servings}$.  
     $\text{Calories} = 2 \times 160 = \mathbf{320\text{ calories}}$.
2. **Apply the 5/20 Rule to the Added Sugars and Dietary Fiber:**
   * *Added Sugars ($32\%\text{ DV}$):* **HIGH** (since $32\% \ge 20\%$).
   * *Dietary Fiber ($4\%\text{ DV}$):* **LOW** (since $4\% \le 5\%$).
   * *Health Evaluation:* This cereal is high in added refined sugars and poor in fiber; not a healthy daily breakfast choice.

### Scenario 2: 4-4-9 Macronutrient Calorie Math
**A turkey sandwich provides:**
* $40\text{ g Carbohydrate}$
* $25\text{ g Protein}$
* $10\text{ g Fat}$

1. **Calculate total calories:**
   * Carbs: $40 \times 4 = 160\text{ cal}$
   * Protein: $25 \times 4 = 100\text{ cal}$
   * Fat: $10 \times 9 = 90\text{ cal}$
   * Total = $160 + 100 + 90 = \mathbf{350\text{ calories}}$.
2. **What percentage of the calories come from carbohydrates?**
   * $\frac{160}{350} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{45.7\%}$ (Within the healthy $45\text{--}65\%$ range).

## 💡 Coach Scott's Top 5 Test-Day Tips
1. **Never forget the Servings Per Container multiplier!** Whenever you read a label question, check whether the prompt asks for "1 serving" or "the entire container / 2 servings".
2. **Memorize 4-4-9 without hesitation:** 4 calories per gram of Carb, 4 for Protein, 9 for Fat.
3. **Know the 5/20 benchmark:** $\le 5\%$ is LOW; $\ge 20\%$ is HIGH.
4. **Link specific nutrients to their diseases:**
   * Sodium $\rightarrow$ Hypertension
   * Saturated/Trans Fat $\rightarrow$ Atherosclerosis & Heart Disease
   * Calcium/Vitamin D $\rightarrow$ Osteoporosis
   * Added Sugar/Refined Carbs $\rightarrow$ Type 2 Diabetes
   * Iron deficiency $\rightarrow$ Anemia
5. **Remember the 3,500 calorie weight balance rule:** 1 lb of fat = 3,500 calories. A daily deficit of 500 calories drops 1 lb per week.
