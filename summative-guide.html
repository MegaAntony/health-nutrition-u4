The 6 Nutrients & The 4-4-9 Calorie Math

Your body needs six essential nutrients to survive. Three are Macronutrients (they provide usable caloric energy). Three are Micronutrients / Regulators (they have 0 calories, but drive every metabolic process).

Carbohydrates 4 Cal / g
Body's Primary Fuel

Main energy source for brain & muscles. Recommended: 45%–65% of daily calories.

Sources: Oats, brown rice, sweet potatoes, fruits, whole wheat.
Proteins 4 Cal / g
Growth & Tissue Repair

Builds muscle, skin, antibodies, and enzymes. Recommended: 10%–35% of daily calories.

Sources: Chicken, eggs, fish, lentils, beans, tofu, Greek yogurt.
Fats (Lipids) 9 Cal / g
Long-Term Storage & Insulation

Absorbs fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). Recommended: 20%–35% of daily calories.

Sources: Olive oil, avocados, almonds, salmon, flaxseeds.

The 3 Micronutrients & Vital Regulators

Vitamins (Organic)

• Water-Soluble: B-complex & Vitamin C (excreted in urine; daily intake needed).
• Fat-Soluble: A, D, E, K (stored in fat tissues & liver; excess can be toxic).

Minerals (Inorganic)

Elements from the earth.
• Calcium: Bones, teeth, muscle contraction.
• Iron: Hemoglobin in red blood cells.
• Sodium: Fluid balance (keep < 2,300 mg).

Water (Most Essential)

Makes up 60%–70% of body mass. Regulates body temperature via sweating, lubricates joints, transports nutrients, flushes metabolic toxins.

Label analysis is guaranteed to appear on Coach Scott's test. You must be able to calculate multi-serving quantities and immediately spot healthy vs. unhealthy nutrient profiles.

* The % Daily Value tells you how much a nutrient in a serving contributes to a daily diet of 2,000 calories.

🎯 The Golden 5/20 Rule for %DV

≤ 5% Daily Value
Is considered LOW in that nutrient.
≥ 20% Daily Value
Is considered HIGH in that nutrient.
🔻 Nutrients to keep LOW (≤ 5%): Saturated Fat, Trans Fat, Sodium, Added Sugars.
🔺 Nutrients to keep HIGH (≥ 20%): Dietary Fiber, Vitamin D, Calcium, Iron, Potassium.
📜 Ingredient Order Law

Ingredients are listed strictly in descending order by weight. The ingredient with the highest mass comes first. If high fructose corn syrup or sugar appears in the top 3 items, the food is heavily processed.

The 3 Parts of a Whole Grain Kernel

When grains are refined (like white flour or white rice), the outer bran and inner germ are stripped away, leaving only empty starchy endosperm.

1. The Bran (Outer Shell)

Contains dietary fiber, B-vitamins, and trace minerals. Keeps digestion smooth and lowers LDL cholesterol.

2. The Endosperm (Middle)

The largest portion containing carbohydrates and some protein. This is the only part left in refined white flour.

3. The Germ (Inner Embryo)

Nutrient-dense powerhouse containing Vitamin E, healthy fats, and antioxidants.

Metabolism, Energy Balance & 3,500 Calorie Rule

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)

The baseline calories burned at complete rest to keep organs alive (heart pumping, lungs breathing, brain functioning). Accounts for ~60–75% of daily energy burn.

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

The total calories burned in 24 hours: BMR + Thermic Effect of Food (digestion) + Physical Activity.

⚖️ The 3,500 Calorie Weight Rule

1 pound of body fat = approximately 3,500 calories.

• To lose 1 lb per week: Create a deficit of 500 calories/day ($3,500 \div 7 = 500$).
• Safe Medical Guideline: Lose no more than 1 to 2 pounds per week to avoid muscle loss and metabolic slowing.

Eating Disorders & Body Dysmorphia

Eating disorders are serious psychiatric medical conditions, not lifestyle choices. Know the behavioral markers and physical warning signs:

Anorexia Nervosa
Severe Caloric Restriction

Intense irrational fear of gaining weight, severe body dysmorphia (seeing oneself as overweight despite being dangerously thin).

Health Complications: Bradycardia (slow heart rate), bone density loss, amenorrhea, growth of lanugo (fine hair for warmth), cardiac failure.
Bulimia Nervosa
Bingeing Followed by Purging

Recurrent episodes of consuming large amounts of food followed by self-induced vomiting, laxative abuse, or excessive exercise.

Health Complications: Severe electrolyte imbalance (hypokalemia), fatal heart arrhythmias, tooth enamel erosion from stomach acid, esophageal tears.
Binge Eating Disorder
Binging WITHOUT Purging

Uncontrollable consumption of large quantities of food rapidly until painfully full. Accompanied by intense shame and guilt, but no purging.

Health Complications: Severe obesity, Type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol, hypertension, heart disease.
06

Diet-Related Chronic Diseases

Hypertension
High Blood Pressure
Caused by chronic high Sodium intake (> 2,300 mg/day) and low potassium. Draws excess water into arteries, raising pressure and causing heart failure, stroke, and kidney damage.
Atherosclerosis
Arterial Plaque
Caused by diets high in Saturated Fats & Trans Fats. Elevates LDL cholesterol, which forms hard plaques along arterial walls, causing heart attacks and angina.
Type 2 Diabetes
Insulin Resistance
Caused by excess refined sugars, physical inactivity, and obesity. Body cells become desensitized to insulin, leaving blood glucose dangerously elevated.
Osteoporosis
Brittle Bones
Caused by inadequate Calcium and Vitamin D intake during youth. Bones become porous and prone to fractures. Prevented by calcium, sunlight, and weight-bearing exercise.
